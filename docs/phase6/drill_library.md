# Batting IQ drill library v0.1 (draft)

DRAFT, 17 Nov 2026. Source of truth: `drill_library.json` (this page is made from it). **Cam must sign off every drill** before it goes live: use `cam_drill_signoff.md`. The plan logic is in `drill_plan_rules.md`; the app prompt is `docs/lovable/drill_plan_prompt.md`.

57 drills: three for each fault in rubric v0.3 chain order (F14 is part of F07), plus three for F00 ("Keep it grooved"). Each fault's **beginner** drill is the "Drill to try" on the priority fix card.

## Levels

- **Beginner** (B): New to cricket, or new to batting with a proper ball. Shadow, tee and drop-feed drills.
- **Club** (C): Plays for a school or club team. Drop feeds and underarm throws, with a marker or a partner check.
- **Advanced** (A): Plays first-team adult cricket, or county or academy age-group cricket. Overarm tennis-ball throws, scoring games and phone review.

## Safety lines (shown with every drill)

- **All players:** "Warm up first. Make sure there is space around you and nobody stands within reach of your swing. Use a tennis ball or soft ball for partner drills, with the feeder standing to the side, not in line with the shot. With a hard ball, always wear a helmet with a faceguard and proper protective kit. Stop if anything hurts."
- **Under 18, or age not known:** "Players under 18: do these drills with an adult supervising, and always wear a helmet with a faceguard when batting against a hard ball, in nets and in matches."
- Each drill also has its own one-line safety note (below).

## Design rules

- No drill needs a hard ball. Every partner drill uses a tennis ball. Solo drills use a tennis ball or no ball.
- Feeders and throwers stand to one side of the line of the shot, out of reach of the bat.
- No drill claims to prevent injury, and no wording says the app is as good as a coach.
- Each fault's beginner drill is the 'Drill to try' on the priority fix card (results_tips_and_sharing_prompt.md Pass 1).
- Demo videos: an adult only (Cam), a tennis ball, no club badges, nobody else recognisable in shot, filmed from behind the bowler and side-on, about 20 seconds.

## Words used in the drills

- **Drop feed:** Your partner kneels on your leg side, out of the path of the bat, and drops a tennis ball so it bounces in front of your front pad.
- **Underarm throw:** Your partner throws a tennis ball underarm from 6 to 8 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot.
- **Overarm throw:** Your partner throws a tennis ball overarm, with one bounce, from 10 to 12 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot.
- **Tee:** A batting tee, or a tall cone with a tennis ball on top.
- **Shadow:** The movement with a bat but no ball.

## F01: Head moves too early

Tip: Keep your head still and your eyes level as the bowler runs in, so you get the clearest view of the ball.

### F01-B · Still head, ball drop (beginner)

**What it fixes:** Keeps your head still and your eyes level before the ball comes, because a moving head gives you a blurred view of the ball.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis ball, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 8 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 10 balls.

**Set-up:** Take your stance. Your partner stands 3 metres away, a little to your off side, and holds a tennis ball at shoulder height.

**Steps:**

1. Look at the ball in your partner's hand.
2. Keep your head still and your eyes level.
3. Your partner drops the ball when they choose.
4. Keep your head still until the ball bounces.
5. Play a soft forward defence, so the ball stops near your feet.

**Check:** You know it's working when your partner sees no head movement before the bounce on 8 of 10 balls.

**Next step:** Your partner waits a different time before each drop, so you cannot guess when the ball comes.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. Play soft defence shots only, so the ball stays near your feet.

### F01-C · Run-up watch (club)

**What it fixes:** Trains a still head while the bowler runs in, because that is when most heads start to move.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner, Phone, propped up. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 6 throws.

**Set-up:** Your partner stands 8 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot. Prop a phone 2 metres behind your partner at chest height, pointing at you.

**Steps:**

1. Start the phone recording.
2. Your partner takes five walking steps in and throws a tennis ball underarm.
3. Keep your head still and your eyes level during the steps.
4. Play a straight drive or a forward defence.
5. After 6 balls, watch the clip. Check your head against something fixed behind you, such as a fence post.

**Check:** You know it's working when your head stays level with the same fixed point behind you until the ball leaves your partner's hand.

**Next step:** Your partner jogs in and throws a little faster, so the ball arrives sooner.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands 6 to 8 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, and watches every ball. Put the phone where the ball and the bat cannot reach it.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: the partner walks five steps and throws underarm while Cam's head stays level against the background. Side-on: one rep with the head still during the walk-in, then the drive.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F01-A · Trigger, then still (advanced)

**What it fixes:** Lets you keep your trigger movement but finish it before the ball is released, so your head is still at the moment that matters.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner, Phone, propped up. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 8 throws.

**Set-up:** Your partner throws tennis balls overarm, with one bounce, from 10 to 12 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot. Prop the phone behind your partner, out of the line of the ball.

**Steps:**

1. Make your normal trigger movement as your partner's arm comes up.
2. Finish the trigger before the ball leaves the hand.
3. Keep your head still and your eyes level as the ball is released.
4. Play the shot that suits the ball, or leave it.
5. After 8 balls, watch 3 clips. Check that your head stops before the release.

**Check:** You know it's working when the clips show your trigger finished and your head still at release, and the ball seems to arrive later.

**Next step:** Your partner varies the pace and the length, so you must still finish the trigger early against quicker balls.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands at least 10 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, or behind a net screen. Put the phone where the ball and the bat cannot reach it.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: Cam's small trigger movement, finished and still as the thrower lets go. Side-on: one slow and one normal-pace rep showing the trigger, the still head, then the drive.

## F02: Unbalanced stance

Tip: Stand with your weight even and your knees soft, so you can move forward or back with equal ease.

### F02-B · Ready-position hops (beginner)

**What it fixes:** Sets an even, relaxed stance, so you can move forward or back with equal ease.

**You need:** Bat. **Solo:** yes. **Time:** 6 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 10 reps.

**Set-up:** Stand in an open space in your normal stance, with your feet about shoulder-width apart.

**Steps:**

1. Take your stance.
2. Do two small hops on the spot.
3. Land with your weight even on both feet and your knees soft.
4. Hold still for 3 seconds.
5. Check that you could step forward or back without a wobble.

**Check:** You know it's working when you land in the same balanced stance every time, with no shuffle of the feet.

**Next step:** After the hold, step straight into a shadow forward defence, then a shadow back-foot defence.

**Safety:** Clear a space. Make sure nobody is within two bat lengths of your swing.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: Cam hops twice, lands in a balanced stance and holds. Side-on: the same, showing soft knees and the weight over the middle of the feet.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F02-C · Forward or back call (club)

**What it fixes:** Tests your stance under a call, because a balanced stance lets you go either way quickly.

**You need:** Bat, Partner, or a phone playing calls you recorded. **Solo:** yes. **Time:** 8 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 10 calls.

**Set-up:** Take your stance. Your partner stands 3 metres away, out of reach of your bat. Or record yourself saying "forward" and "back" in a random order, with gaps, and play it back.

**Steps:**

1. Hold your stance with your weight even.
2. On "forward", play a shadow front-foot drive.
3. On "back", play a shadow back-foot defence.
4. Return to your stance and hold it for 2 seconds.
5. Wait for the next call.

**Check:** You know it's working when you move as quickly forward as back, and you never need a small step before you go.

**Next step:** Your partner calls later, just as they pretend to let go of the ball.

**Safety:** Clear a space. Make sure nobody is within two bat lengths of your swing.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: Cam reacts to "forward" and "back" calls with shadow shots and returns to the same stance. Side-on: the held stance and one call each way.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F02-A · Freeze on release (advanced)

**What it fixes:** Checks your balance at the moment the ball is released, when balance matters most.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 12 balls.

**Set-up:** Your partner throws tennis balls overarm, with one bounce, from 10 to 12 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot.

**Steps:**

1. Your partner runs in and either throws, or fakes the throw and calls "freeze".
2. On a throw, play the right shot.
3. On "freeze", stop still at once.
4. On a freeze, lift one foot a little and check that you stay balanced.
5. Mix 8 throws and 4 freezes in a random order.

**Check:** You know it's working when you can lift either foot on every freeze without falling to one side.

**Next step:** Your partner throws a little faster and fakes later, so your balance must hold under more pace.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands at least 10 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, or behind a net screen.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: one throw played, then one fake where Cam freezes and lifts a foot without a wobble. Side-on: the freeze, showing even weight and soft knees.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

## F17: Head, hands and feet not lined up as the bowler lets go

Tip: As the bowler lets go, line up your head, hands and feet over off stump, so you start on the line of the ball.

### F17-B · Line up on off stump (beginner)

**What it fixes:** Lines up your head, hands and feet over off stump as the ball is released, so you start on the line of the ball.

**You need:** Bat, Stump or cone, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 6 minutes. **Reps:** 10 reps.

**Set-up:** Put a stump or a cone on your off-stump line at the crease. Your partner stands 10 metres away, where a bowler lets go of the ball.

**Steps:**

1. Take your stance.
2. Set your feet first, then your hands, then your head.
3. Your partner pretends to bowl.
4. As their arm comes over, line up your head, hands and feet over off stump.
5. Your partner calls "yes" or "no" after each rep.

**Check:** You know it's working when your partner calls "yes" on 8 of 10 reps.

**Next step:** Your partner throws a soft underarm tennis ball after the check, and you play a forward defence.

**Safety:** No ball is needed. If you add a ball, use a tennis ball thrown underarm from the side of the pitch.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: film over the partner's shoulder as Cam lines up head, hands and feet over off stump at a pretend release. Side-on: Cam sets the feet, then the hands, then the head.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F17-C · Phone line-up check (club)

**What it fixes:** Lets you check your line-up on your own, with the phone as the bowler's eye.

**You need:** Bat, Phone, propped up, Cone or stump. **Solo:** yes. **Time:** 8 minutes. **Reps:** 10 reps.

**Set-up:** Prop the phone at chest height 10 metres away, straight down the pitch. Put a cone on your off-stump line at the crease.

**Steps:**

1. Start the phone recording.
2. Take your stance.
3. Say "now" when an imaginary bowler lets go.
4. Hold your position for 2 seconds after "now".
5. Do 10 reps, then watch the clip.
6. Pause on each "now" and check your head, hands and feet against the cone.

**Check:** You know it's working when your head, hands and feet stack over the cone in the paused clip on most reps.

**Next step:** Add a shadow drive after each hold, and check that your head stays on the line.

**Safety:** Clear a space. Make sure nobody is within two bat lengths of your swing.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler (from the phone's position): Cam holds the line-up at "now", with the off-stump cone in view. Side-on: Cam says "now" and holds still.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F17-A · Line up and leave (advanced)

**What it fixes:** Joins the line-up to judging the ball, so you know where off stump is and leave well.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner, Cone. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 8 throws.

**Set-up:** Put a cone on your off-stump line at the crease. Your partner throws tennis balls overarm, with one bounce, from 10 to 12 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot. They mix balls on the stumps with balls wide of off stump.

**Steps:**

1. Line up your head, hands and feet over off stump as your partner's arm comes over.
2. Drive the balls that are full and on the stumps.
3. Leave the balls that are wide of off stump.
4. Hold your line-up for one second on every leave.
5. Your partner calls "yes" if you were lined up at release.

**Check:** You know it's working when you get 8 "yes" calls in 10 balls and leave the wide ones with confidence.

**Next step:** Your partner adds balls just outside off stump, so the leave is a closer decision.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands at least 10 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, or behind a net screen.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: Cam lined up over off stump, driving one full ball and leaving one wide ball. Side-on: the line-up held still on the leave.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

## F13: Hands late: no rhythm from the backlift

Tip: Start lifting the bat a little earlier, as the bowler gets ready to let go, so the swing has time to flow.

### F13-B · Lift on "now" (beginner)

**What it fixes:** Starts your backlift earlier, so the swing has time to flow and is not rushed.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 8 minutes. **Reps:** 10 throws.

**Set-up:** Your partner throws tennis balls underarm from 6 to 8 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot.

**Steps:**

1. Take your stance.
2. Your partner says "now" as their throwing arm starts to swing.
3. Start to lift your hands on "now".
4. Play a straight drive or a forward defence.
5. Check that your bat was up before the ball bounced.

**Check:** You know it's working when your bat is up and ready before the ball bounces, with no rush.

**Next step:** Your partner stops saying "now", and you start the lift on the swing of their arm alone.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands 6 to 8 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, and watches every ball.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: the partner says "now" as the arm swings, and Cam starts the backlift on the word. Side-on: the bat up and ready before the bounce, then the drive.

### F13-C · Lift with the gather (club)

**What it fixes:** Times your backlift to the bowler's action, not to the ball, so it starts at the same moment every time.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 10 balls.

**Set-up:** Your partner stands 10 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, and bowls a tennis ball with a short, gentle action.

**Steps:**

1. Do 3 shadow reps with no ball first.
2. Watch your partner's bowling action.
3. Start to lift your hands as their front arm goes up.
4. Have the bat up by the time their bowling arm comes over.
5. Play the shot that suits the ball.

**Check:** You know it's working when your backlift starts at the same point in the action every ball, and the shot feels unhurried.

**Next step:** Your partner bowls a little quicker, and you keep the same early start.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The bowler bowls gently from at least 10 metres and moves out of the line of the shot after letting go.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: Cam's hands start to lift as the bowler's front arm goes up. Side-on: the backlift starting with the gather and the bat up as the bowling arm comes over.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F13-A · Early lift, quicker ball (advanced)

**What it fixes:** Keeps an early, unhurried backlift against quicker balls, where late hands cost the most.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner, Phone, propped up. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 6 throws.

**Set-up:** Your partner throws tennis balls overarm, with one bounce, from 10 to 12 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot. Prop the phone side-on to you, out of the line of the ball.

**Steps:**

1. Start the lift as your partner's arm comes up.
2. Have the bat up by the time the ball bounces.
3. Swing in one movement into the shot.
4. Drive the full balls and defend the rest.
5. After every 6 balls, watch one clip. Check that the bat was up before the bounce.

**Check:** You know it's working when the clips show the bat up before the bounce, even on the quicker balls.

**Next step:** Your partner adds a quicker ball every third throw, with no warning.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands at least 10 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, or behind a net screen. Put the phone where the ball and the bat cannot reach it.

## F03: Hands go out away from the body

Tip: Lift your hands up and back, close to your body, with your top hand in charge; it does not matter if the bat points towards the slips.

### F03-B · Mirror backlift (beginner)

**What it fixes:** Teaches a backlift that goes up and back, close to your body, with the top hand in charge.

**You need:** Bat, Mirror or a dark window. **Solo:** yes. **Time:** 6 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 10 reps.

**Set-up:** Stand side-on to a mirror, about 2 metres away, so you can see yourself in your stance.

**Steps:**

1. Take your stance.
2. Lift your hands slowly up and back, close to your body.
3. Keep your top hand in charge.
4. Check in the mirror that your hands stay near your back hip.
5. Lower the bat and repeat.

**Check:** You know it's working when your hands stay close to your body on every lift. The bat pointing towards the slips is fine.

**Next step:** After each lift, swing through a slow shadow drive, and check that your hands come down close to your body too.

**Safety:** Swing slowly. Keep the bat at least one bat length away from the mirror or window.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Side-on: Cam's slow backlift, with the hands going up and back close to the body. Behind the bowler: the same lift, with the hands staying close to the back hip.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F03-C · Top-hand tee drives (club)

**What it fixes:** Builds a strong top hand, which keeps the backlift close to your body and the bat straight.

**You need:** Bat (a light or short one is best), Tennis balls, Tee or tall cone. **Solo:** yes. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 8 balls.

**Set-up:** Put a tennis ball on a tee or a tall cone next to where your front foot lands. Face a net or open space.

**Steps:**

1. Hold the bat in your top hand only. Put your bottom hand behind your back.
2. Lift the bat up and back, close to your body.
3. Step in and drive the ball gently off the tee.
4. Do 8 balls with the top hand only.
5. Then do 8 balls with both hands, with the top hand still in charge.

**Check:** You know it's working when the two-handed drives feel led by the top hand and the ball goes straight.

**Next step:** Do the same with soft drop feeds instead of the tee.

**Safety:** Hit into a net or towards open space, away from people, windows and cars. Use a light bat. Stop if your wrist or forearm hurts.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Side-on: Cam drives a tennis ball off a tee with the top hand only, then with both hands. Behind the bowler: the backlift staying close to the body.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F03-A · Net-side lift (advanced)

**What it fixes:** Shows you at once if your hands push out, because the bat touches the netting.

**You need:** Bat, Net lane, Tennis balls, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 2 sets of 8 throws.

**Set-up:** In a net lane, take guard so the off-side netting is about one metre in front of your toes. Your partner throws underarm from 8 metres, from the leg side of the lane.

**Steps:**

1. Do 5 slow shadow backlifts. The bat must not touch the netting.
2. Your partner throws tennis balls on middle stump.
3. Lift up and back, close to your body.
4. Drive straight back down the lane.
5. If the bat touches the netting, do 2 slow shadow lifts before the next ball.

**Check:** You know it's working when the bat never touches the netting and your drives go straight back down the lane.

**Next step:** Your partner throws overarm with one bounce, a little quicker.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands on the leg side of the lane, at least 8 metres away.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: Cam takes guard close to the off-side netting, lifts without touching it, then drives a throw straight. Side-on: the lift staying close to the body.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

## F19: Drove a ball that was too short

Tip: Drive only when the ball is pitched up; if it is short, go back or leave it.

### F19-B · Full or short? (beginner)

**What it fixes:** Trains you to pick the length early, so you drive only balls that are pitched up.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 8 minutes. **Reps:** 12 throws.

**Set-up:** Your partner throws tennis balls underarm from 6 to 8 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot. They throw some full and some short, in a random order.

**Steps:**

1. Watch where the ball bounces.
2. Say "full" or "short" out loud as it bounces.
3. Drive the full balls.
4. Go back to the short balls, or leave them.

**Check:** You know it's working when you choose the right shot on 10 of 12 balls.

**Next step:** Your partner adds balls of a middle length, so the choice is harder.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands 6 to 8 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, and watches every ball.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: Cam drives a full ball, then goes back to a short ball. Side-on: the call "full" or "short" and the matching foot movement.

**Cam sign-off:** pending

### F19-C · Cone on a length (club)

**What it fixes:** Gives you a clear marker for a ball you can drive, so you learn which lengths to drive.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner, Cone or shoe. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 12 throws.

**Set-up:** Put a cone about 2 metres in front of the batting crease, at the side of the pitch so the ball cannot hit it. Your partner throws tennis balls overarm, with one bounce, from 10 to 12 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot.

**Steps:**

1. Watch where the ball bounces, compared with the cone.
2. If it bounces between the cone and you, drive it.
3. If it bounces before the cone, go back and defend, or leave it.
4. Your partner calls "right" or "wrong" after each ball.

**Check:** You know it's working when your partner calls "right" on 10 of 12 balls.

**Next step:** Move the cone 30 centimetres closer to you, so only the fuller balls count as balls to drive.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands at least 10 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, or behind a net screen.

**Demo video to film (status: needed):** Behind the bowler: the cone at the side of the pitch; one ball that bounces past it is driven, one short of it is defended. Side-on: Cam's front foot goes forward only for the full ball.

### F19-A · Drive, defend or leave (advanced)

**What it fixes:** Joins length and line choices, so you drive the right ball and a short one does not catch you out.

**You need:** Bat, Tennis balls, Partner. **Solo:** no, needs a partner. **Time:** 10 minutes. **Reps:** 12 throws.

**Set-up:** Your partner throws tennis balls overarm, with one bounce, from 10 to 12 metres, standing to one side of the line of the shot. They mix full, short and wide balls.

**Steps:**

1. Drive the full balls on the stumps.
2. Go back and defend the short balls.
3. Leave the wide balls outside off stump.
4. Score 1 point for each right choice.
5. Play 12 balls and keep your score.

**Check:** You know it's working when you score 10 or more out of 12, two sessions in a row.

**Next step:** Your partner throws a little quicker, so you have less time to choose.

**Safety:** Use a tennis ball. The thrower stands at least 10 metres away, to one side of the line of the shot, or behind a net screen.
