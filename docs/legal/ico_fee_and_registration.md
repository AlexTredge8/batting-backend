## The short answer

- **Yes, Batting IQ must pay.** It stores and analyses personal data (videos of people, account details) on computers for a business purpose, and none of the exemptions fits (below).
- **Tier 1 (micro organisation): £52 a year**, or **£47 by direct debit** (a £5 discount) (VERIFIED (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/3) regs 3(1)(a) and 3(5); VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/)).
- **It is overdue.** The app already takes uploads, so the fee should be paid **now**, not at launch. **HIGH RISK** until paid, because not paying is a breach of the law with a fixed penalty (below), and because the privacy notice needs the registration number.

## Why Batting IQ must pay

Every organisation or sole trader that processes personal data must pay the fee unless exempt (Data Protection (Charges and Information) Regulations 2018, as amended by S.I. 2025/63 from **17 February 2025**: VERIFIED (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/3); VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/changes-to-the-data-protection-fee/)).

**The exemptions** cover processing **only** for (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/exemptions/)):
- staff administration;
- advertising, marketing and public relations;
- accounts and records;
- not-for-profit purposes;
- personal, family or household affairs;
- maintaining a public register;
- judicial functions;
- processing personal information without an automated system such as a computer;
- (and members of the House of Lords, elected representatives and prospective representatives).

Batting IQ processes customers' videos and account details to **provide its service** and to **train its model**. That is outside every exemption. **Not exempt.**

## Which tier

| Tier | Who (VERIFIED (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/3) reg 3(2)) | Fee from 17 Feb 2025 (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/changes-to-the-data-protection-fee/)) |
|---|---|---|
| **1: micro organisations** | Turnover of **£632,000 or less** in the financial year, **or** **no more than 10** members of staff (also charities and small occupational pension schemes) | **£52** |
| 2: small and medium | Turnover up to £36 million, or up to 250 staff | £78 |
| 3: large | Everyone else | £3,763 (CORRECTED: the "£3,726" figure in one search extract was wrong) |

**Batting IQ: tier 1.** One owner plus a coach helping, and turnover far below £632,000. Only one of the two tests needs to be met. Staff and turnover are judged on the first day of the fee year (reg 3(4)).

**The direct debit discount** is £5, so **£47** (VERIFIED (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/3) reg 3(5)).

## What happens if you don't pay

The ICO can issue a fixed penalty: **£400** for tier 1 (£600 tier 2, £4,000 tier 3), up to **£4,350** with aggravating factors (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/our-information/fixed-penalties-for-failure-to-pay-the-data-protection-charge/); the maximum of £4,350 is also on https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/penalties/). The ICO also publishes a register of fee payers, so non-payment is easy to spot.

## Who registers: decide this first

The fee is paid by the **legal person** that controls the data:
- **If Batting IQ is not a company:** Alex registers as a sole trader, for example "Alex Tredgett, trading as Batting IQ".
- **If Batting IQ Ltd exists:** the company registers, with its Companies House number.
- **If you incorporate later:** the company needs its own registration; cancel the sole-trader one at the same time (VERIFIED in part (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/faqs-data-protection-fee-payment-and-online-registration/): "Separate fees must be paid for each company individually if it is a data controller"; the cancel step is UNVERIFIED).
- **One fee covers everything that person does.** If Alex also coaches for money as the same sole trader, the one fee covers both (VERIFIED in part (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/faqs-data-protection-fee-payment-and-online-registration/): one fee covers all sites of the same legal entity).

The ICO publishes the controller's **name and address** on its public register, plus the registration reference, tier, payment and expiry dates and any trading names. It does not publish the names of the contact people (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/information-we-will-collect-and-publish/)). **CORRECTED (same URL):** the address you give is not a free choice. For a company it should be the registered office. For a sole trader it should be your **principal place of business in the UK**. If that is your home, your home address will be published. If you do not want that, the clean way is a company with a registered office address elsewhere, or a genuine business address.

1. **Check you need to pay (optional, 2 minutes).** Use the ICO self-assessment at https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee-self-assessment/. Expected answer: yes, tier 1 (VERIFIED that the tool exists).
2. **Check the amount.** Same section. If it is not £52 (or £47 by direct debit), use the ICO's figure and change this file.
3. **Gather what the form asks for** (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/register/) and (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/information-we-will-collect-and-publish/)):

   - number of staff and annual turnover (to confirm tier 1);
   - a short description of what you do (UNVERIFIED that the form asks for it);
   - a data protection contact (Alex; no DPO is required);
   - card details, if paying by card.
4. **Start the form.** Go to https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/register/ and choose "Start your new registration application". It takes about 15 minutes and must be done in one session (VERIFIED, same URL).
5. **Pay.** By card online, or by direct debit (£5 cheaper, renews automatically). For a first registration the ICO sends direct debit details when you contact it to register (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/paying-the-data-protection-fee/)). Direct debit is recommended so it does not lapse.
6. **Save the confirmation.** The ICO usually sends confirmation the next working day (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/register/)). Your **registration reference** begins with **Z, A or C** (CORRECTED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/faqs-data-protection-fee-payment-and-online-registration/); the draft said "Z"). A registration certificate can be downloaded from ico.org.uk ("Download registration certificate" link). Save both with the DPIA.
7. **Add the number** to the privacy notice (`training_data_terms_v2.md`, section 1) and the session forms (`consent_wording_v2.md`, Forms A and B).
8. **Check the public register** about seven working days later: the ICO publishes new registrations within seven working days (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/register/)). Search for "Batting IQ" or your name at https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/register-of-fee-payers/ and check the details.
9. **Put a reminder in your calendar** one month before renewal (12 months from payment), even with direct debit, to check the tier and details are still right.
10. **Keep details up to date.** Tell the ICO if the address, trading name, contact or tier changes. The ICO has a "change" page for this (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/change/; its content was not read: UNVERIFIED).

## Other notes

- **Beware of agencies and scam letters** that offer to pay the fee for you, often for more. They have no official standing. Pay only on ico.org.uk (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/faqs-data-protection-fee-payment-and-online-registration/)).
- **The fee is not a licence.** Paying it does not make the app compliant. The DPIA, notice, consent flow and processor contracts still need doing (`dp_review.md`). The ICO says the same: exempt or not, you must still comply (VERIFIED (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/exemptions/)).
